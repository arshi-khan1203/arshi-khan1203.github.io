Explanatory Document

Explainable AI (XAI) refers to the set of methods and principles that make the outputs and decisions of AI models understandable to humans. As large language models like GPT, Claude, Gemini, and LLaMA are deployed in high-stakes domains such as healthcare, finance, and law, the ability to interpret and justify their decisions becomes critical. This presentation highlights the core challenges that make LLMs difficult to explain, including their black-box architecture, reliance on post-hoc explanations, embedded data biases, and the growing gap between model scale and interpretability. It also examines how validation techniques, such as cross-validation, adversarial testing, and human evaluation, combined with performance metrics like precision, recall, F1 score, and ROC-AUC, play a key role in building trust in AI systems by providing measurable evidence of model reliability and fairness.

The second focus of this artifact is current industry efforts to close the explainability gap. Organizations like Anthropic (Constitutional AI), OpenAI (chain-of-thought prompting and RLHF), Google (attention visualization and PAIR tools), and Meta (open-source release and red-teaming) are each taking distinct approaches to improve transparency and accountability. The presentation uses a clean card-based layout to make these complex topics accessible to non-technical stakeholders, reflecting the belief that effective AI communication is as important as technical performance. Design choices prioritized visual clarity, consistent color coding by theme, and concise language to ensure the content is both informative and immediately comprehensible.
